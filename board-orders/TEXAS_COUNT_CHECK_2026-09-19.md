# Texas count check: is 172 LPC agreed orders the whole story? (2026-09-19)

Companion to `TEXAS_VERIFICATION_2026-09-18.md`. That pass downloaded the
Behavioral Health Executive Council (BHEC) Council packets and found 172 LPC
agreed orders (171 files) for 166 people across 19 fiscal quarters, FY21 Q4
to FY26 Q3. The user found that low for 36,214 LPCs and 7,710 LPC Associates.
This pass re-downloaded all 17 Council packets (October 2021 to June 2026),
read every quarterly Enforcement Status Report, performance-measure sheet and
year-end enforcement chart in them, read the LPC board's own 33 meeting files
(September 2020 to May 2026), and pulled the Sunset Advisory Commission and
Health Professions Council figures for the years before BHEC existed. All
packets were deleted after reading; nothing binary is committed.

Fiscal years run September to August: FY25 Q1 is September to November 2024.

## Short answer

1. **171 distinct LPC agreed orders is the right count for what the Council
   packets carry.** Every packet was recounted and every quarter matches the
   manifest. Two entries are duplicate copies of one order (Ronald Saenz,
   FY21 Q4, and William Elkins, FY23 Q2), so 172 orders is really 171
   distinct orders for 166 people. The unbookmarked FY22 Q2 pages hold
   exactly one LPC order (Cynthia Catherine Kay).
2. **The Council packets are not the only online source.** The LPC board's
   own meeting packets for February, May and September 2021 carry the LPC
   agreed orders for FY21 Q1, Q2 and Q3 (September 2020 to May 2021): 55
   orders that the downloader never saw. They are listed by name below.
   Nothing earlier than September 2020 is online.
3. **BHEC's own counts show what the packets miss.** BHEC reported 453
   "agreed orders signed" for all four boards from FY21 Q4 to FY26 Q2; the
   packets for those quarters hold 323 orders for all professions (71
   percent). Part of the gap is that BHEC counts complaints, not orders (one
   order often settles several complaints), part is psychology orders that
   are frequently left out of the packets, and part is default orders and
   contested-case (SOAH) orders that never appear in packets. BHEC does not
   publish an LPC-only discipline count for any year after FY2017.
4. **Texas's formal discipline rate for LPCs is genuinely low.** The packets
   give about 30 LPC agreed orders a year for FY22 to FY25 against roughly
   38,000 LPC licenses, about 0.8 per 1,000 licensees per year. Colorado's 40
   orders a year for 20,000 LPCs is 2.0 per 1,000. Even if the packets miss a
   quarter of the LPC orders, Texas is at about half of Colorado's rate. The
   pre-BHEC years were lower still (12 to 38 LPC disciplinary actions a year
   in FY2013 to FY2017). Texas resolves most complaints without a public
   order: warning letters, conditional letters of agreement and dismissals
   are the normal outcome, and only 9 to 18 percent of resolved complaints
   end in a disciplinary action.

## 5. Plain-language answer

**Is 172 the right count?** Yes for the Council packets, with two small
corrections: it is 171 distinct orders (Saenz and Elkins are each in twice)
for 166 people, and FY22 Q2 adds exactly one (Kay). Every quarter's recount
matches the manifest, and no LPC order was found outside the bookmarked or
foldered sections. But the Council packets only start with the June 2021
quarter. The LPC board's own packets carry 55 more LPC agreed orders for
September 2020 to May 2021, so the true online total is 226. The downloader
should be extended to the three 2021 LPC board packets (section 2b gives the
pages).

**What is missing and how much?**

- Before September 2020: nothing online. Sunset's figures put LPC discipline
  at 12 to 38 actions a year in FY2013 to FY2017, so a full FY2013 to FY2020
  series would be roughly 150 to 200 orders, obtainable only by Public
  Information Act request.
- Default orders and SOAH final orders: never in the packets. BHEC reports 1
  to 6 contested cases heard at SOAH per quarter for all four boards (about
  8 to 14 a year), and 9 to 19 pending cases "going to SOAH" in 2021 and
  2022. Default orders are not reported at all. For LPCs, expect low single
  digits per year of each.
- Orders signed but not in the packet: BHEC's complaint-based count of 453
  for FY21 Q4 to FY26 Q2 against 323 orders in the packets. Multi-complaint
  orders explain some of it (Briggs, Mabray, Franklin and Paugh each settled
  four or more complaints), missing psychology sections explain more, and the
  rest is unexplained. BHEC never gives an LPC-only order count after
  FY2017, so the LPC share of the gap cannot be measured from its figures.
  If the LPC gap is like the all-board gap after allowing for psychology,
  the packets hold about 80 to 90 percent of LPC agreed orders.
- Non-disciplinary outcomes: warning letters, conditional letters of
  agreement and corrective-action dismissals are not orders and are not
  public. They outnumber disciplinary actions three to one in the FY2017
  Sunset table and about five to one in the FY24 to FY26 status reports
  (dismissals against orders).

**Is Texas's rate genuinely low?**

| Currently licensed people flagged with any disciplinary history (LPC.csv) | 301 of 43,924, 6.9 per 1,000 | |

Texas is at roughly half of Colorado's rate on any of these readings, and
the pre-BHEC years were no higher. The reason is not a shortage of
complaints: the LPC board received 462 complaints in FY2025, about 12 per
1,000 licensees, and LPCs supply 55 to 60 percent of BHEC's complaints while
holding 44 percent of its licenses. It is the resolution mix. Only 9 to 18
percent of resolved complaints end in a disciplinary order across the
boards; the rest are dismissed, most with a warning letter, insufficient
evidence, lack of jurisdiction or corrective action taken. So the 171 (now
226) orders are a faithful picture of formal public discipline of Texas
LPCs, and the low number is a real feature of the Texas system rather than
a collection failure. The one genuine collection gap is the 55 orders in
the 2021 LPC board packets.
